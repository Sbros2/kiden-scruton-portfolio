## An engine concept of my own
CRCE is my engine-design project. The current design record is a SolidWorks assembly supported by individual component models, making the relationship between the moving mechanism and its surrounding structure the focus of this page.

## Design concept: counter-rotating crankshafts
My concept is a four-cylinder engine with two separate crankshafts rotating in opposite directions. I think of it as two paired V-twin groups, with a proposed 180° phase relationship between the crankshafts. The CAD views show the packaging exploration; the phase relationship remains a design intention to resolve through kinematic analysis.

The aim is to balance opposing rotational reactions at the engine mounts. My sketch marks equal opposing moments, M₁ = M₂, as the idealized target. Achieving that would depend on matching the time-varying loads and inertia of both groups, not simply choosing opposite rotation directions.

## Where I want to apply it
**High-torque vehicles:** I’m interested in whether the arrangement could reduce the engine’s contribution to chassis twist during hard acceleration or pulling loads. A tractor-pull launch is my motivating example. Driveline and tire reactions would still need to be included in a whole-vehicle model.

**Aircraft:** I want to investigate whether reduced engine reaction torque could reduce roll-correction demands in a single-engine installation. Any resulting reduction in trim drag is a hypothesis to test. Engine balancing alone does not establish cancellation of propeller torque or other aerodynamic effects.

## Next analysis: vibration and time-dependent deformation
The next step is to connect the CAD assembly to a dynamic model: define the crank phasing and coupling, apply representative cylinder loads, and compare the reaction moments transmitted to the mounts throughout a cycle. I want to examine vibration, torsional response, and time-dependent deformation of the supporting structure.

The comparison should include unequal loading, imperfect phasing, and the effect of the output drivetrain. These studies would show when the counter-rotating arrangement helps and what loads remain. They are planned analyses, not completed simulation results.

## CAD record
The downloadable assembly includes the block, pistons, connecting rods, crank, pins, and retaining parts. It is the current design record; dynamic analysis is the next step.
